# 1. How to Make Wudu

*Category: purification · 8 steps · ~5 min · id: `wudu`*


> A calm, step-by-step wudu guide from the Qur'an and the Prophet's practice.


### Step 1 — Begin with intention and Bismillah

Make the intention in your heart that this wudu is for purification before prayer. Say Bismillah before you start. The intention is not spoken out loud.


> **Arabic:** بِسْمِ اللَّهِ

> **Transliteration:** *Bismillah.*

> **Translation:** In the name of Allah.


**Evidence:**
- Quran 5:6 — **Qur'an** (graded by Quran) — _Allah commands washing for prayer in this verse._
- Sunan Abi Dawud 101 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Albani) — _Used for mentioning Allah's name at the beginning of wudu._


### Step 2 — Wash both hands

Wash both hands up to the wrists, starting with the right. The Sunnah description mentions washing the hands three times before the rest of wudu.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 159 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _Uthman described the Prophet's wudu, beginning with washing the hands._


### Step 3 — Rinse the mouth and nose

Rinse your mouth, then draw water gently into the nose and blow it out. Do this with care, especially if fasting.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 159 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _The narration includes rinsing the mouth and nose in the Prophet's wudu._


### Step 4 — Wash the face

Wash the whole face, from the hairline to the chin and from ear to ear. Make sure water reaches the usual face area without harshness.



**Evidence:**
- Quran 5:6 — **Qur'an** (graded by Quran) — _The verse names washing the face as part of wudu._
- Sahih al-Bukhari 159 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _The Sunnah description mentions washing the face three times._


### Step 5 — Wash the arms to the elbows

Wash the right arm up to and including the elbow, then the left arm up to and including the elbow.



**Evidence:**
- Quran 5:6 — **Qur'an** (graded by Quran) — _The verse names washing the hands and arms up to the elbows._
- Sahih al-Bukhari 159 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _The Sunnah description mentions washing the forearms to the elbows._


### Step 6 — Wipe the head

Wet your hands and wipe over the head. Many scholars include the ears with the head; keep this simple and follow the Sunnah calmly.



**Evidence:**
- Quran 5:6 — **Qur'an** (graded by Quran) — _The verse names wiping the head._
- Sahih al-Bukhari 159 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _The Sunnah description includes wiping the head._


### Step 7 — Wash the feet to the ankles

Wash the right foot up to and including the ankle, then the left. Let water reach between the toes and avoid leaving dry spots.



**Evidence:**
- Quran 5:6 — **Qur'an** (graded by Quran) — _The verse names washing the feet to the ankles._
- Sahih al-Bukhari 159 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _The Sunnah description mentions washing the feet to the ankles._


### Step 8 — Finish with the shahadah

After completing wudu, say the shahadah. If your wudu was at a bathroom sink, say it after leaving the toilet area when possible.


> **Arabic:** أَشْهَدُ أَنْ لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ، وَأَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا عَبْدُ اللَّهِ وَرَسُولُهُ

> **Transliteration:** *Ashhadu an la ilaha illa Allah, wa anna Muhammadan 'abdullahi wa rasuluh.*

> **Translation:** I testify that none has the right to be worshipped except Allah, and that Muhammad is the servant of Allah and His Messenger.


**Evidence:**
- Sahih Muslim 234a — **Sahih** (graded by Muslim) — _The narration mentions this remembrance after wudu._

# 2. How to Make Ghusl

*Category: purification · 7 steps · ~6 min · id: `ghusl`*


> The full bath for major ritual impurity, taught through the Prophet's Sunnah.


### Step 1 — Make the intention

Intend in your heart to lift major ritual impurity. You do not need to say the intention out loud.



**Evidence:**
- Quran 5:6 — **Qur'an** (graded by Quran) — _The verse commands full purification when in a state of janabah._
- Sahih al-Bukhari 1 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari and Muslim) — _Actions are by intentions._


### Step 2 — Wash the hands

Begin by washing both hands. This keeps the rest of the ghusl clean and follows the prophetic description.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 248 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _Aishah described the Prophet beginning ghusl by washing his hands._


### Step 3 — Remove impurity

Wash any physical impurity from the private area and anywhere else it reached. Cleanliness comes before the full wash.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 249 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _Maymunah described the Prophet washing away impurity during ghusl._


### Step 4 — Make wudu

Make wudu as you would for prayer. If you are standing where water gathers, you may delay washing the feet until the end.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 248 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _The Prophet made wudu as part of ghusl._
- Sahih al-Bukhari 249 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _Maymunah's report mentions washing the feet after moving from the place of ghusl._


### Step 5 — Wet the roots of the hair

Let water reach the roots of the hair, then pour water over the head. The Sunnah description mentions three handfuls over the head.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 248 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _The narration mentions moving water through the roots and pouring water over the head three times._


### Step 6 — Wash the whole body

Wash the rest of the body so water reaches all skin. Be attentive to folds, underarms, behind ears, between toes, and anywhere water may miss.



**Evidence:**
- Quran 5:6 — **Qur'an** (graded by Quran) — _The verse commands full purification from janabah._
- Sahih al-Bukhari 248 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _The narration ends with pouring water over the whole body._


### Step 7 — Leave clean and ready

If you completed ghusl and did not break wudu during or after it, the ghusl includes purification for prayer. If you did break wudu, make wudu again before praying.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 248 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _The Prophet's ghusl included wudu within it._

# 3. How to Make Tayammum

*Category: purification · 5 steps · ~3 min · id: `tayammum`*


> Dry purification when water is unavailable or harmful to use.


### Step 1 — Use it only when needed

Tayammum is for when you cannot find water or cannot safely use it. If water becomes available and you can use it, return to wudu or ghusl.



**Evidence:**
- Quran 5:6 — **Qur'an** (graded by Quran) — _The verse permits tayammum when water is not found._
- Sahih al-Bukhari 344 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _The Prophet directed a person in janabah without water to use clean earth._


### Step 2 — Make the intention

Intend purification for prayer in your heart. Tayammum is worship, so begin deliberately and calmly.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 1 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari and Muslim) — _Actions are by intentions._


### Step 3 — Touch clean earth once

Place both hands lightly on clean earth, dust, sand, stone, or a clean earthy surface. If there is excess dust, shake or blow it off lightly.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 347; Sahih Muslim 368a — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari and Muslim) — _Ammar's narration describes one light strike of the earth._


### Step 4 — Wipe the face

Wipe over the face once with the dusted hands.



**Evidence:**
- Quran 5:6 — **Qur'an** (graded by Quran) — _The verse names wiping the face for tayammum._
- Sahih al-Bukhari 341 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _The Prophet said wiping the face and backs of the hands was sufficient._


### Step 5 — Wipe the hands

Wipe the hands once. Keep it simple: face and hands, without turning tayammum into a full wudu.



**Evidence:**
- Quran 5:6 — **Qur'an** (graded by Quran) — _The verse names wiping the hands for tayammum._
- Sahih al-Bukhari 347; Sahih Muslim 368a — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari and Muslim) — _Ammar's narration describes wiping the hands and face._

# 4. How to Pray — Core Structure

*Category: prayer · 10 steps · ~10 min · id: `prayer-core`*


> The shared structure of salah, from takbir to taslim.


### Step 1 — Prepare for salah

Make sure the prayer time has entered, you have purification, your body and place are clean, and you face the qibla. Make the intention in your heart.



**Evidence:**
- Quran 4:103 — **Qur'an** (graded by Quran) — _Prayer is prescribed at appointed times._
- Quran 5:6 — **Qur'an** (graded by Quran) — _Purification is commanded when rising for prayer._
- Sahih al-Bukhari 1 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari and Muslim) — _Actions are by intentions._


### Step 2 — Start with takbir

Stand if you are able, raise your hands if following that Sunnah, and say Allahu Akbar to enter the prayer.


> **Arabic:** اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ

> **Transliteration:** *Allahu Akbar.*

> **Translation:** Allah is greater.


**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 757 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _The Prophet taught the man to begin prayer with takbir._


### Step 3 — Recite Quran while standing

Recite Surah al-Fatihah in each rak'ah. In the first two rak'ahs, recite another passage after al-Fatihah when appropriate.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 756 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _No prayer is valid without the Opening of the Book._
- Sahih al-Bukhari 776 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _Abu Qatadah described reciting al-Fatihah and another surah in the first two rak'ahs._


### Step 4 — Bow in ruku

Say Allahu Akbar and bow until you are settled. Keep your back calm and say the ruku remembrance.


> **Arabic:** سُبْحَانَ رَبِّيَ الْعَظِيمِ

> **Transliteration:** *Subhana Rabbiyal-'Azim.*

> **Translation:** Glory be to my Lord, the Magnificent.


**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 757 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _The Prophet taught bowing until calm._
- Sunan Abi Dawud 874; Sahih Muslim 772 — **Sahih** (graded by Muslim and al-Albani) — _Used for the ruku remembrance._


### Step 5 — Rise from ruku

Rise until standing straight and settled. Say the Sunnah phrases for rising and praising Allah.


> **Arabic:** سَمِعَ اللَّهُ لِمَنْ حَمِدَهُ
رَبَّنَا وَلَكَ الْحَمْدُ

> **Transliteration:** *Sami'a Allahu liman hamidah. Rabbana wa laka al-hamd.*

> **Translation:** Allah hears the one who praises Him. Our Lord, and to You belongs all praise.


**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 757 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _The Prophet taught rising until standing straight._
- Sahih al-Bukhari 789 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _Used for the phrases said when rising from ruku._


### Step 6 — Prostrate in sujud

Say Allahu Akbar and prostrate until you are settled. Prostrate on the known points of prostration and say the sujud remembrance.


> **Arabic:** سُبْحَانَ رَبِّيَ الْأَعْلَى

> **Transliteration:** *Subhana Rabbiyal-A'la.*

> **Translation:** Glory be to my Lord, the Most High.


**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 757 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _The Prophet taught prostrating until calm._
- Sunan Abi Dawud 874; Sahih Muslim 772 — **Sahih** (graded by Muslim and al-Albani) — _Used for the sujud remembrance._


### Step 7 — Sit between the two prostrations

Rise from sujud, sit calmly, ask Allah for forgiveness, then make the second sujud.


> **Arabic:** رَبِّ اغْفِرْ لِي

> **Transliteration:** *Rabbi-ghfir li.*

> **Translation:** My Lord, forgive me.


**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 757 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _The Prophet taught sitting between prostrations until calm._
- Sunan Abi Dawud 874 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Albani) — _Used for the supplication between the two prostrations._


### Step 8 — Stand for the next rak'ah

After the second sujud, stand for the next rak'ah if more remain. Repeat the same core structure with calmness.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 757 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _The Prophet said to do this throughout the prayer._
- Sahih al-Bukhari 631 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _Pray as you have seen the Prophet pray._


### Step 9 — Sit for tashahhud

In the sitting place of the prayer, recite the tashahhud. In the final sitting, send salah on the Prophet ﷺ before ending.


> **Arabic:** التَّحِيَّاتُ لِلَّهِ...

> **Transliteration:** *At-tahiyyatu lillah...*

> **Translation:** All greetings, prayers, and pure words are for Allah...


**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 831 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _Ibn Mas'ud narrated the tashahhud taught by the Prophet._
- Sahih al-Bukhari 3370 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari and Muslim) — _Used for sending salah on the Prophet ﷺ in the final sitting._


### Step 10 — End with taslim

End the prayer by saying salam to the right and then to the left.


> **Arabic:** السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ وَرَحْمَةُ اللَّهِ

> **Transliteration:** *As-salamu 'alaykum wa rahmatullah.*

> **Translation:** Peace and Allah's mercy be upon you.


**Evidence:**
- Sunan Abi Dawud 997 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Albani) — _Used for the wording and direction of taslim._
- Sahih al-Bukhari 631 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _The overall prayer is learned from the Prophet's practice._

# 5. Differences per Prayer

*Category: prayer · 5 steps · ~5 min · id: `prayer-differences`*


> Rak'ah counts and the main audible/silent differences across the five prayers.


### Step 1 — Know the rak'ah counts

Fajr is 2 rak'ahs. Dhuhr is 4. Asr is 4. Maghrib is 3. Isha is 4. Keep the core structure the same; the count changes.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 350; Sahih al-Bukhari 1090 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _Aishah narrated that prayers were first prescribed as two rak'ahs, then resident prayers were completed._
- Sahih al-Bukhari 1109 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _Ibn Umar's travel narration mentions Maghrib as three and Isha as two when shortened in travel._


### Step 2 — Fajr is two audible rak'ahs

Fajr has two rak'ahs. The recitation is audible in congregation, and both rak'ahs include al-Fatihah and another recitation.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 891 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _The Prophet's Friday Fajr recitation shows audible recitation in Fajr._
- Sahih al-Bukhari 776 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _Abu Qatadah described recitation in Fajr and the first two rak'ahs of other prayers._


### Step 3 — Dhuhr and Asr are four silent rak'ahs

Dhuhr and Asr are four rak'ahs. In the first two, recite al-Fatihah and another passage; in the last two, recite al-Fatihah. Their recitation is silent.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 776 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _Abu Qatadah described the recitation pattern of Dhuhr and Asr._


### Step 4 — Maghrib is three rak'ahs

Maghrib has three rak'ahs. In congregation, the first two are audible, then the third is silent. Sit for tashahhud after the second and third rak'ah.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 1109 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _The narration mentions Maghrib as three rak'ahs._
- Sahih al-Bukhari 765 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _Reports of the Prophet's Maghrib recitation show audible recitation in Maghrib._


### Step 5 — Isha is four rak'ahs

Isha has four rak'ahs. In congregation, the first two are audible, then the last two are silent. Sit after the second and final rak'ah.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 1090 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _Aishah's narration anchors resident prayers being completed beyond the original two rak'ahs._
- Sahih al-Bukhari 769 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _Reports of Isha recitation show audible recitation in Isha._

# 6. Dhikr After Prayer (Adhkar as-Salah)

*Category: prayer · 7 steps · ~8 min · id: `dhikr-after-prayer`*


> A simple post-prayer routine, aligned with the existing Du'as library.


### Step 1 — Begin with istighfar and peace

After salam, say Astaghfirullah three times, then Allahumma Antas-Salam. This is already available in Du'as under After Prayer Adhkar.


> **Arabic:** أَسْتَغْفِرُ اللَّهَ
اللَّهُمَّ أَنْتَ السَّلَامُ، وَمِنْكَ السَّلَامُ

> **Transliteration:** *Astaghfirullah. Allahumma anta as-Salam, wa minka as-salam.*

> **Translation:** I seek Allah's forgiveness. O Allah, You are peace, and from You is peace.


**Evidence:**
- Sahih Muslim 591 — **Sahih** (graded by Muslim) — _This is the opening remembrance after the obligatory prayer._


### Step 2 — Say the tawhid remembrance

Say the post-prayer tawhid phrases: Allah alone owns dominion and praise; none can withhold what He gives or give what He withholds.


> **Arabic:** لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَحْدَهُ لَا شَرِيكَ لَهُ

> **Transliteration:** *La ilaha illallahu wahdahu la sharika lah.*

> **Translation:** None has the right to be worshipped except Allah alone, without partner.


**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 844 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _This remembrance appears after the obligatory prayer._
- Sahih Muslim 594a — **Sahih** (graded by Muslim) — _Includes the sincere tawhid wording after prayer._


### Step 3 — Make tasbih, tahmid, and takbir

Use a Sunnah count set from the Du'as library. The common version is Subhanallah 33, Alhamdulillah 33, Allahu Akbar 33, then the closing tawhid phrase once.


> **Arabic:** سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ
الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ
اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ

> **Transliteration:** *Subhanallah. Alhamdulillah. Allahu Akbar.*

> **Translation:** Glory be to Allah. All praise is for Allah. Allah is greater.


**Evidence:**
- Sahih Muslim 597a — **Sahih** (graded by Muslim) — _The narration gives the post-prayer count and closing phrase._


### Step 4 — Recite Ayat al-Kursi

Recite Ayat al-Kursi once after each obligatory prayer. The full Arabic is available in Du'as > After Prayer Adhkar.


> **Arabic:** آيَةُ الْكُرْسِيّ

> **Transliteration:** *Ayat al-Kursi.*

> **Translation:** Quran 2:255.


**Evidence:**
- Quran 2:255 — **Qur'an** (graded by Quran) — _The verse itself is Quranic._
- An-Nasa'i, al-Sunan al-Kubra 9848 — **Sahih** (graded by Ibn Hibban and others) — _Used by scholars for reciting Ayat al-Kursi after obligatory prayers._


### Step 5 — Recite the three protective surahs

Recite al-Ikhlas, al-Falaq, and an-Nas. The existing Du'as library keeps the full text and the Fajr/Maghrib count note.


> **Arabic:** قُلْ هُوَ اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ
قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ الْفَلَقِ
قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ النَّاسِ

> **Transliteration:** *Qul huwa Allahu ahad. Qul a'udhu bi-rabbil-falaq. Qul a'udhu bi-rabbin-nas.*

> **Translation:** Surah al-Ikhlas, Surah al-Falaq, and Surah an-Nas.


**Evidence:**
- Sunan Abi Dawud 1523 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Albani) — _Used for reciting the Mu'awwidhat after every prayer._


### Step 6 — Add the Fajr and Maghrib dhikr

After Fajr and Maghrib, add the specific tawhid dhikr ten times. Keep it tied to those two prayers rather than all five.


> **Arabic:** لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَحْدَهُ لَا شَرِيكَ لَهُ

> **Transliteration:** *La ilaha illallahu wahdahu la sharika lah.*

> **Translation:** None has the right to be worshipped except Allah alone, without partner.


**Evidence:**
- Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3474; Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3534 — **Hasan** (graded by at-Tirmidhi and al-Albani) — _Used for the Fajr and Maghrib specific ten-count dhikr._


### Step 7 — Keep it beloved, not burdensome

These adhkar are beautiful Sunnah routines after the obligatory prayer. Do what you can consistently, and return gently when you forget.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 6465 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari and Muslim) — _The most beloved deeds are those done consistently, even if small._

# 7. Mistakes in Prayer & Sujud al-Sahw

*Category: prayer · 7 steps · ~8 min · id: `sujud-sahw`*


> Practical decisions for common prayer mistakes without panic.


### Step 1 — Do not panic

A mistake in prayer does not mean you are hopeless or that the whole prayer is automatically lost. Pause, identify what happened, and fix what can be fixed.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 1229 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _The Prophet corrected a shortened prayer and made sujud al-sahw._


### Step 2 — If you ended early

If you gave salam and then realized you missed a rak'ah, stand, complete what was missed, say salam, then make two prostrations of forgetfulness and finish.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 1229 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _In the Dhul-Yadayn incident, the Prophet completed the missing rak'ahs and made sujud al-sahw._


### Step 3 — If you are unsure of the count

If you cannot tell whether you prayed three or four, build on what you are certain of: take the lower number, complete the prayer, then make two prostrations before salam.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih Muslim 571 — **Sahih** (graded by Muslim) — _The hadith instructs discarding doubt and building on certainty._


### Step 4 — If one number is more likely

If you strongly think one count is correct, act on what seems correct, complete the prayer, say salam, then make two prostrations and say salam again.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 401; Sahih Muslim 572 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari and Muslim) — _The narration teaches seeking what is correct and then prostrating for forgetfulness._


### Step 5 — If you added something

If you accidentally added an extra standing, sitting, ruku, sujud, or rak'ah, return to the prayer order when you notice and make sujud al-sahw.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih Muslim 572 — **Sahih** (graded by Muslim) — _Sujud al-sahw covers mistakes in the prayer and is tied to completing the prayer correctly._


### Step 6 — If you forgot the first tashahhud

If you stood up after the second rak'ah and forgot the first tashahhud, continue if you are already upright, then make sujud al-sahw before salam.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 1224 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _The Prophet stood after two rak'ahs without sitting, then made two prostrations before salam._


### Step 7 — How to perform sujud al-sahw

Make two prostrations like the normal sujud of prayer, with takbir as you go down and rise. Follow the before-salam or after-salam placement for the mistake type you are applying.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 1229 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _The narration describes two prostrations similar to the prayer's prostration._
- Sahih Muslim 571 — **Sahih** (graded by Muslim) — _Mentions two prostrations before salam in the uncertainty case._

# 8. Tawbah — Coming Back to Allah

*Category: foundations · 6 steps · ~6 min · id: `tawbah`*


> Repentance without despair: stop, return, repair, and keep walking.


### Step 1 — Start with hope

Do not begin tawbah by deciding you are too far gone. Allah calls the servants who exceeded limits not to despair of His mercy.


> **Arabic:** لَا تَقْنَطُوا مِنْ رَحْمَةِ اللَّهِ

> **Transliteration:** *La taqnatu min rahmatillah.*

> **Translation:** Do not despair of Allah's mercy.


**Evidence:**
- Quran 39:53 — **Qur'an** (graded by Quran) — _The verse is the central hope anchor for returning to Allah._


### Step 2 — Stop the sin

When you recognize the sin, leave it. If you fall again later, do not use that fall as proof that tawbah was fake; repent again and keep returning.



**Evidence:**
- Quran 66:8 — **Qur'an** (graded by Quran) — _Allah commands sincere repentance._


### Step 3 — Feel regret without self-destruction

Regret means your heart is alive. Let it move you back to Allah, not into despair or hiding.



**Evidence:**
- Sunan Ibn Majah 4252 — **Hasan** (graded by al-Albani) — _Used for the meaning that regret is repentance._
- Quran 39:53 — **Qur'an** (graded by Quran) — _Regret should not become despair of mercy._


### Step 4 — Resolve not to return

Make a real decision to leave the sin. Remove easy paths back to it where you can, and ask Allah for firmness.



**Evidence:**
- Quran 66:8 — **Qur'an** (graded by Quran) — _Sincere repentance includes a serious turning back to Allah._


### Step 5 — Repair people's rights

If the sin harmed another person, return rights, seek forgiveness where appropriate, or repair the harm in the safest truthful way.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 2449 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari) — _The hadith urges settling injustices before the Day when wealth will not settle them._


### Step 6 — Follow with good deeds

After tawbah, move. Pray, give, apologize, delete the path back to the sin, call someone good, or do one quiet act Allah loves.



**Evidence:**
- Quran 25:70 — **Qur'an** (graded by Quran) — _Allah mentions repentance, belief, and righteous deeds._
- Sahih Muslim 2675 — **Sahih** (graded by Muslim) — _Allah is pleased with the repentance of His servant._

# 9. Coming Back to Prayer After Time Away

*Category: foundations · 7 steps · ~7 min · id: `coming-back-to-prayer`*


> Duhaa's signature guide for returning to salah with hope, not guilt.


### Step 1 — You are not forsaken

The fact that you want to come back is a mercy. Begin from hope: Allah did not close the door on you.


> **Arabic:** مَا وَدَّعَكَ رَبُّكَ وَمَا قَلَىٰ

> **Transliteration:** *Ma wadda'aka rabbuka wa ma qala.*

> **Translation:** Your Lord has not abandoned you, nor has He become hateful.


**Evidence:**
- Quran 93:3 — **Qur'an** (graded by Quran) — _Surah Ad-Duhaa is Duhaa's hope anchor._
- Quran 39:53 — **Qur'an** (graded by Quran) — _Do not despair of Allah's mercy._


### Step 2 — Start with today's next prayer

Do not wait for a perfect Monday, Ramadan, or a clean emotional state. Check the next prayer time and pray that prayer.



**Evidence:**
- Quran 4:103 — **Qur'an** (graded by Quran) — _Prayer is tied to appointed times._
- Sahih Muslim 684c — **Sahih** (graded by Muslim) — _When a prayer is missed through sleep or forgetfulness, pray it when remembered._


### Step 3 — Aim for the five, one prayer at a time

The goal is all five daily prayers. The next step is the next salah in front of you. Let one prayer become the bridge to the next.



**Evidence:**
- Quran 2:238 — **Qur'an** (graded by Quran) — _Allah commands guarding the prayers._
- Sahih al-Bukhari 8; Sahih Muslim 16 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari and Muslim) — _The five daily prayers are among Islam's central obligations._


### Step 4 — Handle missed prayers carefully

For prayers missed through sleep or forgetfulness, pray them when you remember. For a long period away from prayer, scholars discuss details differently; do not freeze. Repent, protect today's prayers, and ask a trusted scholar for a practical qada plan for your case.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 597; Sahih Muslim 684c — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari and Muslim) — _The explicit text covers sleep and forgetfulness; broader long-term qada details require scholarly review._
- Quran 66:8 — **Qur'an** (graded by Quran) — _Begin with sincere repentance._


### Step 5 — Make the next prayer easy to reach

Put a prayer mat where you see it, keep wudu when you can, set gentle notifications, and choose a simple surah you know. Reduce friction without turning worship into pressure.



**Evidence:**
- Sahih al-Bukhari 6465 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Bukhari and Muslim) — _The most beloved deeds are those done consistently, even if small._


### Step 6 — If you miss again, come back again

A missed prayer is serious, but it is not an invitation to miss the next one. Make tawbah, pray what needs praying, and return without theatrics.



**Evidence:**
- Quran 39:53 — **Qur'an** (graded by Quran) — _Do not despair of Allah's mercy._
- Sahih Muslim 2675 — **Sahih** (graded by Muslim) — _Allah is pleased with repentance._


### Step 7 — Ask Allah to help you pray

End with asking Allah for help. The prayer life you want is built by Allah's help, then your next honest step.


> **Arabic:** اللَّهُمَّ أَعِنِّي عَلَى ذِكْرِكَ، وَشُكْرِكَ، وَحُسْنِ عِبَادَتِكَ

> **Transliteration:** *Allahumma a'inni 'ala dhikrika, wa shukrika, wa husni 'ibadatik.*

> **Translation:** O Allah, help me to remember You, thank You, and worship You well.


**Evidence:**
- Sunan Abi Dawud 1522 — **Sahih** (graded by al-Albani) — _The Prophet taught Mu'adh this supplication after prayer._
